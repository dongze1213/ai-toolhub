Color Theory Basics

Complementary colors sit opposite each other on the color wheel and create maximum contrast. Analogous colors are neighbors on the wheel and create harmonious, low-contrast schemes. Triadic colors are evenly spaced at 120-degree intervals, offering vibrant variety while maintaining balance.

FAQ

▶ What is a color palette?
A color palette is a collection of colors used together in design. Good palettes follow color theory principles like complementary, analogous, or triadic harmony to create visually pleasing combinations.
▶ What are color harmony modes?
Color harmony modes use color theory to generate pleasing combinations: Complementary uses opposite colors, Analogous uses adjacent colors, Triadic uses three equally spaced colors, and Split-complementary uses a base color plus two adjacent to its complement.
▶ How do I choose colors for my project?
Start with your brand or primary color, then use a palette generator to find harmonious accent colors. Consider contrast for accessibility, and test your palette on real UI components to see how it looks in context.
